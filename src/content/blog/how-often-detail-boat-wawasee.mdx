If you boat out of Syracuse, you've already discovered the bad news: Lake Wawasee is hard on boats. The water's clean, but the sun is direct, the pollen falls in May like green snow, and the boats that don't get cared for show it by year three.

Here's what we recommend for almost every pontoon, runabout, ski boat, or wakeboard boat we touch — and why.

## Three touch points a year

Most lake boats need detailing **three times per season**:

1. **Spring de-winterize** (late April / early May). Pulls the boat out of its winter cover or shrink wrap, gets the dust off, freshens the vinyl, and sets you up for the year.
2. **Mid-season maintenance wash** (late June / early July). Catches the pollen and bug bodies before they bake on. Just a wash plus wipe-down — usually under two hours dockside.
3. **Fall winterize + detail** (October). The most important one. Heads off the mildew that lives all winter under shrink wrap.

If you only do one of the three, do the fall one.

## Why fall matters most

A pontoon that goes into storage clean comes out of storage clean. A pontoon that goes in with dock spit, sunscreen residue, and a wet carpet creates its own ecosystem under the wrap until April. We've pulled covers off boats that needed a full re-detail before they ever touched the water — that's a $500 problem you create yourself.

A fall detail typically includes:

- Hull rinse, wash, and oxidation cut on faded gel-coat
- Vinyl seat clean + UV-protectant conditioner
- Carpet shampoo and full extraction (this is where the mildew starts)
- Metal polish on cleats and rails
- Bilge wipe-down
- Bow and stern light check

## The pontoon people: a special case

Pontoons are the most common boat on Wawasee, and they're the boats most affected by the &ldquo;just a quick wash&rdquo; school of maintenance.

The deck carpet on a pontoon is essentially an outdoor rug that lives wet half the year. It needs extraction — not just vacuuming — at least once per season. The aluminum logs and fences accumulate water-line oxidation that you can polish off easily in year two but not so easily in year five.

If you own a pontoon and you have time for one optional add-on, get the **aluminum log polish** at fall winterize. The lift posts hide most of the damage all summer; you don't see it until the boat's out of the water in October.

## What we do that an at-home wash doesn't

Three things:

1. **Marine-safe degrease**. Standard car soap strips marine wax. The product matters.
2. **Gel-coat oxidation cut**. Faded clear coat doesn't come back without compound and polish. A regular wash makes it worse — fine micro-abrasions take more clear off.
3. **Extraction, not just shampooing.** Wet carpet stays wet. Extraction removes the water and the gunk. Critical before storage.

## On-lift vs. on-trailer

We strongly prefer detailing pontoons on the lift. You don't risk hull damage from trailering twice. The fenders and rails are accessible at human height instead of overhead. And we don't tie up your weekend pulling the boat in and out.
